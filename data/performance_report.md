# AMARO Strategy Evaluation: Cost-Adjusted Backtest, Stress Test, Alpha Test

Generated by `src/report.py` / `src/monte_carlo.py` / `src/alpha_test.py` from
real `data/ledger.csv` trade history and real market data.

**Bottom line up front: this strategy does not currently show robust,
statistically significant alpha on any of the three Yahoo-sourced assets
when tested rigorously.** It's not worthless -- GBPUSD and XAUUSD both
beat their own drawdown/cost hurdles -- but "profitable in a backtest" and
"has a proven statistical edge" are different claims, and the evidence
below only weakly supports the second one. Details below; recommendations
at the end.

## What changed this round: realistic transaction costs

Every previous report in this project's history used **zero-cost fills**
for GBPUSD/XAUUSD/USDJPY (only the Deriv path had any spread modeled).
That materially inflated every number reported so far. Fixed by adding a
per-symbol cost model (`market_data.COST_PROFILES`) applied at every fill
in `trading_robot.py`:

| Symbol | Spread (round-trip) | Slippage | Commission (per side) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GBPUSD | 0.00015 (~1.5 pips) | 0.01% | 0.005% |
| USDJPY | 0.015 (~1.5 pips) | 0.01% | 0.005% |
| XAUUSD | $0.35 | 0.01% | 0.010% |
| BTCUSDT | $5.00 | 0.05% | 0.100% |

**These are illustrative retail-ish assumptions, not your actual broker's
fee schedule.** Replace them with real numbers from your broker's
contract specifications before trusting any of this for live capital --
see Recommendations.

**Immediate effect: GBPUSD's raw-mode strategy flips from a nominal
profit ($148.73, zero-cost) to a net loss (-$71.67) once realistic costs
are applied.** Its edge was thin enough (Profit Factor 1.04) that
transaction costs alone erode it. XAUUSD and USDJPY remain profitable
after costs, reduced from their zero-cost figures.

## Alpha / Edge Significance Testing (`src/alpha_test.py`) -- the key section

Two independent tests, because they answer different questions:
- **Edge t-test**: is the average trade's return significantly different
  from zero (breakeven)?
- **Alpha vs. buy-and-hold**: after accounting for the fact this is a
  long-only strategy that's sometimes just holding the same asset a
  buy-and-hold investor holds, is there *residual* return left over --
  i.e. does the entry/exit timing add value beyond simple market
  exposure? This is the more rigorous test, and the one that answers
  "does it actually have an edge, or is it just sometimes in a rising
  market."

| Symbol | Mode | Trade t-test p-value | Edge significant? | Alpha (annualized) | Alpha p-value | Alpha significant? | Strategy CAGR | Buy&Hold CAGR |
|---|---|---:|:---:|---:|---:|:---:|---:|---:|
| GBPUSD | raw | 0.965 | no | +1.12% | 0.442 | no | 1.06% | -0.12% |
| GBPUSD | memory | 0.811 | no | +1.43% | 0.322 | no | 1.37% | -0.12% |
| XAUUSD | raw | 0.053 | no (borderline) | +2.78% | 0.333 | no | 11.91% | **14.24%** |
| XAUUSD | memory | **0.037** | **YES** | +1.13% | 0.683 | no | 10.87% | **14.24%** |
| USDJPY | raw | 0.083 | no | +3.17% | **0.029** | **YES** | 5.48% | 4.28% |
| USDJPY | memory | 0.855 | no | -0.80% | 0.507 | no | 2.48% | 4.28% |

**Read this table carefully -- it's the honest answer to "does this have
an edge":**

1. **XAUUSD memory mode is the only case with a significant trade-level
   edge (p=0.037), but its alpha vs. buy-and-hold is NOT significant
   (p=0.68), and its CAGR (10.87%) is lower than just buying and holding
   gold (14.24%).** During this backtest window gold was in a strong
   uptrend; the strategy's per-trade edge is real in a narrow statistical
   sense, but it didn't translate into beating a passive investor who
   just bought gold and held it. The strategy's value proposition here is
   more "similar returns with less time exposed to the market" than
   "market-beating returns."

2. **USDJPY raw mode is the only case with significant alpha vs. its own
   benchmark (p=0.029)** -- genuinely interesting -- but its trade-level
   t-test wasn't significant (p=0.083), and memory mode makes USDJPY
   worse on every metric. Mixed, not a clean result.

3. **GBPUSD shows no significant edge or alpha by either test**, on
   either mode. Its positive memory-mode PnL survives mostly because
   drawdown/costs were low, not because of a demonstrated statistical
   edge.

4. **None of the three symbols show a strategy that robustly,
   significantly beats its own buy-and-hold benchmark.** This is the
   central, honest finding: this crossover+memory strategy is not
   currently a demonstrated source of alpha. It may still be useful for
   other reasons (lower time-in-market, psychological ease of a
   rules-based system, avoiding buy-and-hold's full drawdown exposure),
   but "proven edge" is not supported by this evidence.

## XAUUSD Out-of-Sample Validation (`src/optimize.py`)

Direct response to the "get an out-of-sample / walk-forward validation"
recommendation above: SMA (fast, slow) periods were grid-searched using
**only** training data (2018-01-01 to 2025-07-01, real XAUUSD candles),
scored by training-period Sharpe with a 15-trade minimum so a lucky
low-sample combo can't win by chance. Grid deliberately small (35
combinations) -- searching a huge space against one split is itself a
form of overfitting. The chosen parameters were then run once, untouched,
against the held-out test period (2025-07-01 to present) that the search
never saw. Baseline (9/21, never tuned) run the same way for comparison.
Each experiment gets its own isolated ledger tag (`XAUUSD_BASE`,
`XAUUSD_OPT`) so their memory systems can't cross-contaminate.

**Selected: SMA(7, 50)**, chosen for the best training Sharpe (0.92 vs.
baseline's 0.57) among eligible combinations.

| Experiment | Mode | Period | Trades | Win % | Profit Factor | Max DD % | Sharpe | Net PnL % |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline (9/21) | raw | train | 49 | 34.7 | 1.87 | 9.63 | 0.57 | 50.50 |
| Baseline (9/21) | raw | test | 6 | 50.0 | 7.51 | 3.40 | 1.57 | 34.19 |
| Baseline (9/21) | memory | train | 49 | 34.7 | 1.87 | 9.63 | 0.57 | 50.50 |
| Baseline (9/21) | memory | test | 3 | 100.0 | undef | 0.00 | -- | 42.16 |
| **Optimized (7/50)** | raw | train | 25 | 52.0 | 4.34 | 2.50 | 0.92 | 55.01 |
| **Optimized (7/50)** | raw | test | 3 | 66.7 | 21.63 | 2.14 | -- | 53.73 |
| **Optimized (7/50)** | memory | train | 25 | 52.0 | 4.34 | 2.50 | 0.92 | 55.01 |
| **Optimized (7/50)** | memory | test | 1 | 100.0 | undef | 0.00 | -- | 68.66 |

(Baseline memory mode's train numbers exactly match raw's -- it recorded
zero SKIPs during 2018-2025, only starting to match past losses once
gold's 2025-2026 rally created tightly-clustered price zones. Not a bug,
just what the decay window did with this particular data.)

**Read this honestly, not optimistically.** Directionally, the tuned
model held up out-of-sample (higher win rate and profit factor than
baseline in both train and test, not a collapse) -- that's mildly
reassuring. But **the test period produced only 1-6 closed trades per
row above, which is too few to compute a reliable Sharpe or draw any
statistically confident conclusion.** This is a structural limitation of
a low-frequency crossover strategy (roughly 6-25 signals over 7.5 years
of training): a single 1-year holdout window is fundamentally too short
to validate it properly, no matter how the parameters are chosen. Treat
this result as "did not obviously fail out-of-sample" rather than
"proven to generalize." A real validation would need either many more
years of forward data, or a walk-forward scheme with multiple
train/test splits across the existing history to get more than a
handful of out-of-sample observations.

## Recommendations Before Considering Live Capital

1. **Replace `market_data.COST_PROFILES` with your actual broker's real
   fee schedule** (spread, commission, swap/overnight financing if
   holding positions multi-day). The numbers above are informed
   estimates, not your broker's contract. If your real costs are higher
   (common for retail accounts, especially on GBPUSD/USDJPY where the
   assumed spread is tight/ECN-like), GBPUSD's edge likely stays negative
   and USDJPY's may follow.

2. **Do not deploy USDJPY memory mode live** -- it underperforms raw mode
   on every metric across every decay window tested. If USDJPY is traded
   at all, use raw mode, and even then note its alpha result (p=0.029)
   doesn't replicate in the trade-level test.

3. **Don't treat XAUUSD's results as proof of skill over a buy-and-hold
   allocation to gold.** If the goal is just gold exposure, a
   buy-and-hold position would have outperformed this strategy over
   2018-present with far less operational complexity. The strategy's
   case has to rest on something buy-and-hold doesn't offer (drawdown
   reduction, capital efficiency from ~40-60% time-in-market, etc.) --
   quantify that trade-off explicitly before deciding it's worth running.

4. **[Done, data-limited] Out-of-sample validation was run** (see the
   section above, `src/optimize.py`) -- the tuned SMA(7,50) model didn't
   collapse out-of-sample, but the 2025-07-present test window only
   produced 1-6 closed trades, nowhere near enough to trust statistically.
   Re-run this as a proper walk-forward (multiple rolling train/test
   splits across 2018-present, not one single split) to get enough
   out-of-sample trade observations to actually trust a conclusion.

5. **This strategy has no explicit stop-loss or take-profit** -- it exits
   purely on the opposite crossover signal, which can be a long time
   after a large adverse move starts. Max Drawdown figures above are
   realized-PnL-only (don't capture intra-trade floating drawdown); a
   real risk-management layer (hard stop-loss, position-level risk cap)
   should be added and backtested before live use, independent of the
   alpha question.

6. **Extend live paper-testing before real capital.** `src/live_monitor.py
   --paper` has been running against Deriv's live feed and is the right
   next step to keep running for weeks/months to see how the strategy
   behaves on data that didn't exist when any of this was built --
   the only true out-of-sample test available.

7. **`src/mt5_executor.py` remains completely unverified** -- it's never
   been run, because the `MetaTrader5` package doesn't install on this
   development machine (Windows-only). Validate the connection on real
   Windows/MT5 hardware, on a demo account, before it's anywhere near
   live capital.
